Topic 1

What Is the Stock Market?

The stock market is an organised marketplace where shares of listed companies are issued, bought and sold. A share is a small unit of ownership in a company. When you hold a share, you own a tiny part of that business and may earn from its growth or from dividends.

Companies sell shares to raise money for expansion, new products or debt repayment. Investors, from individuals to mutual funds, supply that money in the hope of earning returns. In this way, savings are turned into productive investment, which is why the market is closely linked to the wider economy.

In India, trading mainly happens on two exchanges. The Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE), set up in 1875, is Asia's oldest exchange, and its benchmark index is the Sensex. The National Stock Exchange (NSE) started trading in 1994 with fully electronic trading, and its benchmark index is the Nifty 50. Investors place orders through registered brokers, and shares are held in demat accounts.

🌱

Benefits

  • Potential long-term wealth creation
  • Ownership in leading companies
  • Dividends and easy buying or selling
  • Funds for business growth and jobs
⚠️

Risks

  • Share prices can fall as well as rise
  • Capital can be lost, with no guaranteed return
  • Company, economic and global events affect prices
  • Poor research or panic selling hurts results
Topic 2

Primary vs Secondary Market

The two markets work together. One creates new securities and the other lets investors trade them afterwards.

🏦 Primary Market

Here a company issues shares for the first time, or issues more, directly to investors. It receives the money. Methods include an Initial Public Offering (IPO), Follow-on Public Offer (FPO) and rights issue. Examples: the LIC IPO in 2022 and the Zomato IPO in 2021.

🔄 Secondary Market

Here existing shares are traded between investors on NSE and BSE. The company does not receive the money, but the market gives liquidity and fair price discovery. Examples: buying Reliance Industries, TCS or Infosys shares through a broker.

Topic 3

Role of SEBI in India

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is the regulator of India's securities market. It was established in 1988 and was given statutory status under the SEBI Act, 1992, which gave it legal powers to protect investors and regulate the market.

🛡️

Investor Protection

Sets rules to guard small investors from fraud and unfair practices, and runs investor education and grievance redressal such as SCORES.

⚖️

Market Regulation

Regulates stock exchanges, IPOs, mutual funds and listed companies to keep trading orderly.

🔍

Transparency

Requires listed companies to disclose financial results and key events, so investors can decide with facts.

🤝

Intermediaries

Registers and supervises brokers, depositories, merchant bankers and mutual funds.

🚫

Insider Trading

Prohibits trading on unpublished price-sensitive information and can penalise offenders under its insider trading regulations.

Key Takeaways

🏢

Shares mean ownership

Companies raise capital and investors share in growth, with risk.

🔁

Two markets, one system

The primary market issues shares; the secondary market trades them.

🛡️

SEBI keeps it fair

Regulation builds trust, transparency and investor safety.

Conclusion

The stock market connects people who have savings with companies that need capital. The primary market creates new shares, while the secondary market gives investors a place to trade them with liquidity. NSE and BSE provide the platforms, and SEBI, strengthened by the SEBI Act, 1992, protects investors and supports transparency. A basic understanding of these ideas helps students see how markets support economic growth, and why careful research and awareness of risk are essential before investing.
